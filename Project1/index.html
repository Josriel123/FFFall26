Expedition Overview

The Mesozoic Era spanned over 180 million years, giving rise to some of the largest terrestrial predators and herbivores ever to walk the Earth. From warm shallow inland seas to towering prehistoric conifer forests, distinct ecosystems fueled incredible evolutionary adaptations across three major geological periods.

This archive compiles field observations, fossil strata analysis, and biometric telemetry gathered from major excavation basins worldwide. Use the field guide below to examine creature species, compare biological scales, and simulate prehistoric acoustic vocalizations.

Mesozoic Geologic Eras

The three primary time periods defining the Age of Reptiles:

Period 01 252 – 201 MYA

The Triassic Period

Climate: Hot, arid, supercontinent Pangea

Following the Great Permian Extinction, early archosaurs and small bipedal dinosaurs emerged alongside early seed ferns. Terrestrial ecosystems were dominated by dry desert basins and hardy cycad groves.

Period 02 201 – 145 MYA

The Jurassic Period

Climate: Warm, humid, lush rainforest floodplains

Pangea broke apart into Laurasia and Gondwana. Humid sea winds created vast fern floodplains that supported massive long-necked sauropods like Brachiosaurus and armored stegosaurs.

Period 03 145 – 66 MYA

The Cretaceous Period

Climate: Mild, modern continents forming, flowering plants

The pinnacle of dinosaur biodiversity saw advanced apex predators like Tyrannosaurus Rex, horned ceratopsians, and feathered dromaeosaurs thrive right up to the Chicxulub asteroid event.
